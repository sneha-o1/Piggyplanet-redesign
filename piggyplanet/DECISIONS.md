# DECISIONS

## 1. Track and approach

I chose **Part 2 — The Premium Home Page** rather than the Part 1 ingestion/scraper track. The assignment asks candidates to go deep on one track instead of half-assing both, so I focused the available time on building and polishing a complete product experience.

PiggyPlanet was an existing project, which allowed me to spend the time on product presentation, interaction design, responsive behavior, and visual polish rather than creating a superficial concept from scratch.

For the product preview, I used clearly labelled illustrative/demo data rather than fabricated user metrics, testimonials, or company claims.

## 2. Trade-off

The main trade-off was breadth versus polish. Instead of adding many sections or interactions, I focused on a smaller set of experiences that communicate the product clearly: the hero, an interactive product preview, the saving journey, a Daily Surprise interaction, and a final CTA.

With a real week, I would extend the product preview into a more complete interactive prototype, add deeper accessibility testing, test across more devices and browsers, and refine the animation timing based on real user feedback.

## 3. AI usage and verification

I used AI tools during development for brainstorming, implementation assistance, debugging, and reviewing responsive/UI decisions.

I personally integrated the resulting code into the existing Angular/Ionic project, verified the behavior in the browser, checked the page at 390px mobile and 1440px desktop widths, tested the interactions and navigation, and reviewed the content for fabricated claims. I also made the final decisions about the visual direction, demo-data wording, animations, layout, and product presentation.
